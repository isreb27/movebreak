# The evidence behind the defaults

Movebreak's built-in activities and timings follow published research. This page
summarises it; follow the links for the studies. It is not medical advice.

**The headline:** getting up and moving lightly every 30 minutes is the best-supported
habit for people who sit all day. Eye breaks help while you keep doing them. Standing
alone, posture checks and water reminders add little.

| Practice | In Movebreak | What the evidence shows | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short walks every 30 min | Walk break, on | In a Columbia trial, 5 minutes of walking every 30 minutes worked best, cutting post-meal blood-sugar spikes by more than half; every walking dose lowered blood pressure; hourly breaks did little for blood sugar. Small trial (11 people). WHO recommends replacing sitting with activity of any intensity. | Strong |
| Reminder software | The app itself | A 2025 meta-analysis of 18 trials found on-screen prompts cut sitting by about 12.5 minutes and added about 1,000 steps per workday. | Moderate |
| Eye breaks (20-20-20) | Eye break, on | Reminders reduced eye-strain and dry-eye symptoms over two weeks; the benefit was gone a week after they stopped. | Moderate |
| Micro-breaks in general | All reminders | Small gains in vigour and less fatigue; no overall productivity gain. Breaks are framed as wellbeing, not productivity. | Moderate |
| Strength "exercise snacks" | Strength snack, opt-in | A few one-minute vigorous bouts a day are linked to lower mortality (observational). Strengthening helps office neck pain more than stretching. | Moderate |
| Stretching and changing position | Stretch, on | No sitting posture is proven best; holding any posture too long is the problem. | Low to moderate |
| Standing more | Sit–stand, opt-in | Standing more did not lower heart-disease risk and long daily standing was linked to circulatory problems. | Strong (against standing as the fix) |
| Drinking water | Water, opt-in | Thinking suffers only after losing about 2% of body mass in water, which is rare at a desk. | Low for desk reminders |
| Breathing reset | Breathing, opt-in | Five minutes a day of cyclic sighing improved mood more than mindfulness meditation in a one-month trial. | Moderate (one trial) |
| "Sit up straight" checks | Not included | Even physiotherapists disagree on the best sitting posture. | Evidence against |
